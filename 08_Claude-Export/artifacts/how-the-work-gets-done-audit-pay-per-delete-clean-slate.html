The audit

Nobody pays us anything until this is done and you have seen it. It is not a sales call with a score attached. It is an item-by-item examination of three reports, and it is the only honest basis for telling you what a program can and cannot do for you.

What we pull apart

  • Every account on all three reports, inventoried: creditor, account number fragment, date opened, date of last activity, date of first delinquency, balance, status, and the full reported payment grid
  • The three reports compared against each other — an item that reports differently at Experian than at TransUnion is itself evidence of inaccuracy, and it is the single most productive thing an audit finds
  • Duplicate reporting: the same debt appearing twice because it was sold, or appearing as both a charge-off and a collection
  • Re-aged accounts: a date of first delinquency that has been moved forward, which illegally extends how long an item can report
  • Balances, high credit and past-due amounts that don't reconcile with each other or across bureaus
  • Collection accounts reporting without documented chain of ownership
  • Required fields left blank — an incomplete tradeline is a disputable tradeline
  • Hard inquiries with no apparent permissible purpose
  • Personal information variants — name spellings, old addresses, employer data — that create mixed or merged files
  • Public record and court data checked against the actual court record where one exists

The part most companies skip

We build an aging calendar for every derogatory on your file. Most negative information falls off on its own seven years from the date of first delinquency. If an item is dropping off in four months by operation of law, we tell you that, and we do not charge you to remove it. Knowing what is about to expire changes which program makes sense and what it should cost you.

Clean Slate, day by day

The bureaus get thirty days by law and nobody can shorten that. What we can do is refuse to waste a single day of it. Here is the actual calendar.

  1. Enrollment and final strategy

    Day 0

    Audit complete, eligibility confirmed, filings drafted and evidence attached. Nothing about day one is improvised — it is built before the clock starts.

  2. Everything files at once

    Within 24 hours

    Bureau disputes under FCRA §611 to all three, furnisher disputes under §623 direct to each creditor and collector, and debt validation under FDCPA §809(b) on any collector still inside their thirty-day window from first contact. A standard program sends these in sequence. We send them in parallel, which is where most of the time is actually saved.

  3. The statutory window

    Days 1 to 30

    We log the exact date each dispute was received, because that date starts a clock the bureau is legally bound by. A missed deadline is not an annoyance — it is grounds to demand deletion.

  4. First results, and same-day escalation

    Days 30 to 35

    Deletions confirmed against your monitoring. Anything returned as "verified" gets a method-of-verification demand under §611(a)(7) the same day it lands, requiring the bureau to describe how it verified the item and who it spoke to. Most bureaus cannot answer that question well.

  5. Method-of-verification responses

    Days 35 to 45

    A bureau that cannot describe a real procedure has admitted the investigation was inadequate. That admission is the basis for cycle two — and it is leverage a once-a-month dispute schedule never gets to use.

  6. Cycle two

    Days 40 to 50

    New filings on new grounds: procedural failure, inconsistency across the three bureaus, documentation the furnisher failed to produce. Escalation letters go to furnisher compliance departments rather than general dispute intake.

  7. Regulatory escalation

    Day 45

    CFPB complaints filed against any bureau or furnisher that missed a deadline, refused a method-of-verification request, or reinserted a previously deleted item. State Attorney General where the conduct warrants it. These are filed on schedule, not as a threat.

  8. Final pull and closeout

    Days 50 to 60

    Fresh tri-bureau report, a written summary of every item and its outcome, and a call. If anything is still outstanding we tell you exactly what it is and what the remaining options are.

Why we gate it

Volume is the enemy of speed. A file that fires off disputes on thirty items at once gets flagged as frivolous under §611(a)(3), and a flagged file stops being investigated at all — which costs you the exact thing you paid for. Aggressive only works when every filing is specific, documented, and defensible. That is why Clean Slate is capped at roughly twelve derogatory items, why we want your documentation, and why we ask you to hold new applications steady for sixty days. It is also why we will tell you no when the answer is no, and put you in a standard program instead.

Important disclosures

What we are. Full Circle Financial Group is a document preparation firm. We prepare, file and dispute on your behalf using rights available to you under federal law. Every filing is made in your name with your written authorization.

No guarantee of results. We do not promise, guarantee or predict any specific credit score, any score increase, or that any particular item — or all items — will be removed, corrected or updated. Any item on a credit report may be disputed; inaccurate, incomplete, unverifiable and obsolete items carry by far the highest deletion rate, and accurate, verifiable information may remain on your report until it ages off under federal law. Results vary by individual report and depend on the responsiveness of the credit bureaus and data furnishers, and on your own credit activity.

Timelines are estimates. All timeframes on this page are estimates based on statutory investigation windows and prior file experience, not commitments. Credit bureaus are permitted up to 30 days — and in some circumstances 45 days — to complete a reinvestigation. Neither we nor any other company can shorten a statutory window. Where this page describes 45 to 60 days, that refers to our filing and escalation cadence, not a guaranteed deletion date.

The audit. The audit is provided at no charge and carries no obligation. It is an examination of the information reported about you; it is not a score projection, a legal opinion, or a representation that any item will be removed.

Pay per delete. Items are priced per item, per bureau. The written quote issued after your audit is the binding figure; the schedule on this page is the standard rate card it is built from. You are invoiced only for items confirmed removed from your report. Not all reports qualify. A deletion at one bureau does not mean the item is removed from the other two, and some furnishers and bureaus will not negotiate deletion at all.

Clean Slate. Eligibility is determined after your audit and not all reports qualify. Where a report is not a candidate, we will say so and recommend a standard program instead. Clean Slate is $1,850 and Clean Slate Priority is $2,200; both are standalone services and neither is included in the $300, $950, $1,150 or $1,500 programs.

Tradelines. Aged tradelines are authorized-user placements on seasoned accounts. Reporting to the credit bureaus is at the sole discretion of the issuing creditor and is not guaranteed as to timing, duration or score impact. Authorized-user positions do not create liability for the primary account balance. Tradelines are included in the $1,150 and $1,500 programs and in Clean Slate Priority only.

Your rights. You have the right to dispute inaccurate information in your credit report directly with the credit bureaus yourself, at no cost, and to obtain a free copy of your credit report. You will receive a written contract and a statement of your Consumer Credit File Rights Under State and Federal Law before any agreement is signed, and you may cancel that contract without penalty or obligation within three business days of signing.
