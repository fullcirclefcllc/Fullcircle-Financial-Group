01 — Positioning

What You Are Actually Selling

Not documents — attorneys and platforms both sell those, and cheaply. You are selling completion: proof that the plan actually contains the estate.

The whole category has an unfunded-trust problem and no one is selling the fix

Attorneys draft. Advisor platforms generate. Then a family gets a binder and a two-page letter telling them to retitle their own house, call their custodian, and update their beneficiary forms. Most of them never do. The trust controls only what was titled to it, so what those families actually bought was a very expensive document that will not do the thing it was purchased for.

None of the advisor platforms fund anything. EncorEstate, Wealth.com, Vanilla and FP Alpha all generate documents; not one of them records a deed, chases a custodian, or confirms a beneficiary form was acknowledged. That is the entire opportunity, and it is operational work that is not the practice of law in most jurisdictions.

So the product is a funding certificate: every asset, its final title, verified. Plus the annual stewardship that keeps it true when someone gets married, moves, sells a building, or dies. Documents are a transaction. Funding and stewardship are a relationship.

The proof engine

Funding completion rate. Days to zero open items. Designation defects found per household. Stewardship renewal rate. These are operational numbers, they are provable, and they are the only credible proof available in a category where outcome stories are private by nature and testimonials from grieving families are in poor taste.

What you can never say

"The exemption is about to be cut in half" — it is $15M per person, indexed, with no sunset. "A living trust protects your assets from creditors" — it does not. "This protects you from Medicaid" — a revocable trust does nothing there, and that claim is a malpractice magnet. Nothing implying you decide which documents someone needs. And no quasi-legal titles: no "trust advisor", no "estate planning specialist", nothing with "legal" in the trade name.

05 — The org chart of a machine

How This Division Is Actually Staffed

Five pods. The Funding pod is the one that does not exist anywhere else in this industry, and it is the reason clients choose you.

The rule that keeps this legal

Agents inventory, audit, assemble, execute, track and remind. Attorneys decide, draft, and explain. Humans in your firm approve and sign. Every gate approval is logged with approver, timestamp and version; every funding instruction traces to counsel's written direction; and no compensation logic anywhere references a legal fee. In the category with the longest enforcement history in financial services, that log is the warranty on the entire product.

The point of all of it

Everyone drafts. Almost nobody finishes. Finishing is the business.

The documents in this category are close to commodity — good attorneys are plentiful and platforms have driven the price down. What has not been commoditized, and what nobody has industrialized, is the sixty days of unglamorous work between a signature and a plan that actually contains the estate. Own that, prove it with a completion rate, and turn every finished household into an annual relationship. That is a business the platforms cannot copy, because copying it would mean doing the work.
