01 — Findings

The Verdict

The estate planning industry sells documents and then walks away from the only step that makes them work. That abandoned step is an entire business.

Primary findingFunding is the product. Everyone else treats it as homework.

A revocable living trust controls exactly one thing: assets titled to the trust. An unfunded trust produces precisely the outcome the client paid to avoid — probate anyway, no incapacity management because the successor trustee has nothing to manage, gaps between the document and the actual estate that invite heir challenges, and the failure of any tax structuring built on top.

Attorneys draft. Platforms generate. Almost nobody retitles the deed, chases the custodian, fixes the stale beneficiary designation naming an ex-spouse, checks whether the operating agreement even permits assignment of the membership interest, or confirms the S-corp stock went to an eligible shareholder. That work is unglamorous, entirely operational, unambiguously not the practice of law in most jurisdictions — and it is the difference between a plan and a binder.

The second finding is about your marketing, and it is urgent. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act set the federal estate, gift and GST exemption at $15,000,000 per person and $30,000,000 per couple from 2026, indexed, with no sunset. The scheduled reversion to roughly $7M is gone. Every "the exemption is about to be cut in half" campaign in this industry is now selling a lie — and a false urgency claim is exactly the kind of misrepresentation that draws consumer-protection enforcement.

Where the value actually moved

Federal estate tax is now a concern for a very small sliver of households. For everyone else the proposition is probate avoidance, incapacity planning, blended-family and beneficiary control, business succession, basis planning — where a step-up now frequently beats lifetime gifting — and state estate and inheritance taxes, which more than a dozen states impose, several at thresholds far below the federal one. Oregon starts at $1 million; Massachusetts at $2 million.

What probate actually costs

California is the clearest example because its fees are statutory and computed on gross value, and the same schedule is paid twice — once to the attorney and once to the personal representative. That is roughly $26,000 on a $500,000 estate and about $46,000 on $1 million, before filing fees, referee appraisal, publication and bond. And because it is gross-based, a $1.5M home with a $1M mortgage generates fees on $1.5M. Most states use a reasonable-fee standard instead, so those figures are the high end — but nine to twenty-four months and full public record apply everywhere.

The same legal wall governs this division as asset protection. The Florida Supreme Court held that "the assembly, drafting, execution and funding of living trust documents constitute the practice of law" — note that Florida put funding itself on the lawyer's side of the line. So the safe scope for funding work is jurisdiction-specific: clerical execution of an attorney's written instruction is defensible almost everywhere, but deciding what to fund or how to title it is not, and in Florida the whole activity is treated as practice. Confirm your scope state by state before you sell a funding service into one.

The one rule that makes this division work

An engagement is not complete until the funding tracker shows zero open items. Not when documents are signed — when the last deed is recorded, the last account retitled, the last beneficiary form acknowledged by the custodian in writing. Build the tracker as the system of record for the engagement and the entire operating discipline falls out of it: what is open, who is blocking, what the client still has to sign, and what percentage of the estate is actually inside the plan.

Where you sit relative to the advisor platforms

EncorEstate runs $99–$149 a month or $100–$650 per project with no required subscription and a 60-point human quality review. Wealth.com requires an annual subscription, caps "unlimited" plans at 120 clients per advisor licence, and — the detail the market notices — only permits amendments while the advisor stays subscribed. Vanilla licenses per user. FP Alpha runs $1,790–$1,995 a year. All of them describe themselves as scrivener services, a term that carries real regulatory weight and is not a safe harbor by itself. None of them funds anything. That is the gap, and it is the whole opportunity.

The definition of done, and why it has to be strict

An engagement is complete when the funding tracker shows zero open items and a verification pass has confirmed each one against a document or a written custodian acknowledgement. Not when the client says they handled it. Not when a form was mailed. The tracker is the system of record, the funding completion rate is the headline metric, and the funding certificate at handoff is the artifact no competitor in this category produces.

Capacity math

A typical household funding cycle is 8–14 hours of coordination spread over 60 days, most of it chasing custodians and confirming receipt. One trained funding coordinator running a documented tracker handles 90–120 households a year. With ANCHOR generating the tracker, drafting the correspondence and chasing the follow-ups, that moves toward 200. The funding coordinator is the first hire in this division — before a closer, before an assistant.

What the attorney side costs — price beside it, never on top of it

From a 2026 survey of 909 firms, with 94% flat-fee: a standalone power of attorney runs a median of $300, a standalone will $625, a standalone revocable living trust $2,475. Bundled, a will package runs about $1,000 individual and $1,500 for a couple; a trust package about $2,700 individual and $3,000 for a couple. Working ranges: simple will package $800–$1,500; trust package $2,500–$5,000; blended-family or business-owner packages $5,000–$15,000. Within-state variation frequently exceeds state-to-state variation.

Why stewardship is the number to grow

Ongoing maintenance in this market runs $250–$1,500 a year for review plans, with trust amendments at $300–$1,500. A stewardship household costs very little to serve and renews annually — so it compounds in a way engagement volume never does. Two hundred households at $350 is $70,000 a year of revenue that arrives whether or not you sold anything that month.

The nine trust-mill hallmarks regulators name

Free or low-cost seminars and in-home visits targeting seniors. A financial inventory collected under the banner of estate planning, followed by a product pitch on a second visit. Non-lawyer sales staff with quasi-legal titles giving individualized recommendations. A single captive attorney who never meets the client, paid by the company rather than the client, with fees routed through an intermediary. Cookie-cutter templates with no state-law customization. Pressure to sign at the first meeting with no independent review period. Compensation weighted to the product rather than the plan. Marketing built on fear. And misrepresented credentials or guarantees. Design each of those out of the system deliberately, and document that you did.

Privilege is not automatic, and your files are discoverable

Intake gathers a complete financial and family inventory. Work product created before an attorney engagement exists is not privileged — a non-attorney coordinator's files are discoverable, and in the trust-mill cases those files were the evidentiary backbone. Design the system so the privileged and non-privileged record sets are physically separate, and write the retention policy on the assumption that everything on your side may one day be read aloud.

12 — Closing

Closing

What this audit is really saying

Every firm in this category sells the same documents. The documents are not the problem. The problem is that the industry hands a family a binder, tells them to retitle their own house, and calls the engagement complete — and then three years later a probate court sorts out the assets that never made it in.

Own the discovery. Find the stale designations nobody looked at. Hand counsel a finished file. And then fund the plan to zero open items and prove it with a certificate. That work is operational, defensible, and almost entirely unoccupied — and the households you fund become an annual stewardship base that compounds every year you keep it.
