Your rights

Rights you have, whether or not you hire anyone

Federal law gives you these protections. We'd rather you know them before you talk to any credit repair company, including us.

Fair Credit Reporting Act

Free weekly credit reports

You can get free reports from Equifax, Experian and TransUnion every week at AnnualCreditReport.com, the official site for free reports.

Fair Credit Reporting Act

Dispute it yourself, for free

You have the right to dispute inaccurate information with the credit bureaus yourself, at no cost. The bureau generally has 30 days to investigate, or up to 45 days in some cases, such as when you send more information during the investigation.

Credit Repair Organizations Act

Three business days to cancel

You can cancel a credit repair contract within 3 business days of signing, without penalty. The contract must be in writing.

Credit Repair Organizations Act

No payment before the work is done

A credit repair company can't charge you before the services are fully performed. Personal credit repair is never billed upfront. You pay only as the law allows, after the work is done, as spelled out in your written agreement.

Why you need it

Why personal credit matters

The cost of borrowing

Credit history is one of the things lenders use to set interest rates and terms. The same loan can cost very different amounts depending on the profile behind it.

Approvals

Cards, auto loans and mortgages all start with a look at your reports. Errors on them can cost you an approval you would otherwise qualify for.

Insurance and rentals

In many states, insurers may use credit-based insurance scores, and landlords often check credit before approving a lease.

The foundation for funding

Many business lenders still look at the owner's personal credit, especially for newer companies. An accurate, well-managed personal file can support what comes after.

When you need it

When to look at your reports

  • BeforeA mortgage, car or funding application

    Review first, apply second. Errors are easier to address before an application than after a denial.

  • AfterIdentity theft

    Act quickly. You can place a fraud alert or a credit freeze with the bureaus for free, and report the theft at IdentityTheft.gov. Then we go through the reports for accounts that aren't yours.

  • AfterA denial

    Find out why. If you're denied based on your credit report, the lender must tell you which bureau it used, and you can get a free copy of that report within 60 days.

FAQ

Questions clients ask

Do you charge upfront for credit repair?

No. Personal credit repair is never billed upfront. You pay only as the law allows, after the work is done, as spelled out in your written agreement. Under the Credit Repair Organizations Act, a credit repair company can't take payment before the services are fully performed. If you sign up by phone, the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule (16 CFR 310.4(a)(2)) also limits when a fee can be requested.

Can I dispute items myself?

Yes. You can dispute inaccurate information directly with the credit bureaus, for free. Many people do. We offer an organized process and the tracking, but you never have to hire anyone to dispute.

Will you guarantee a score increase or deletions?

No. Nobody can honestly promise a specific score change, deletion or timeline. Results depend on what's on your reports and how the bureaus and creditors respond.

How long do negative items stay on a report?

Most negative information can be reported for up to seven years. A Chapter 7 bankruptcy can be reported for up to ten. Accurate items within those periods can stay.

What if a bureau verifies the item?

If the item is verified as accurate, it generally stays. You can add a brief statement to your file explaining your side, and we'll talk through other options with you.

What's the difference between a hard and soft inquiry?

A hard inquiry happens when you apply for credit and can affect your scores for a time. A soft inquiry, like checking your own reports or a prescreened offer, doesn't affect your scores.

What do I need to get started?

A photo ID, proof of address and access to your three reports. You can get free weekly reports at AnnualCreditReport.com.
