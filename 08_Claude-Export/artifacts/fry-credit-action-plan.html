What we can use from these letters

AThe claimed owner changed in 9 weeks

In April the debt was "owed to SMI – Solar Mosaic Inc." In June it was "owed to Mosaic Funding X LLC." Anyone who claims they bought a debt has to prove the full chain of ownership, back to WebBank, including the account-level assignment. If they can't document it, they can't verify it to the bureaus.

BKelly's two accounts are $39.90 apart

$72,562.51 and $72,522.61, both originated through the same channel. We'll require the signed loan contract for each account. The goal is to confirm these are two real, separate systems, not one project booked twice.

CThe letters are addressed to "Susan Fry"

We'll check that the name, SSN, and address on Kelly's reports match these accounts exactly. Identity mismatches between a collector's file and a bureau file are valid dispute grounds.

DThe balance is frozen as of March 31, 2025

Both collectors show $0 interest, $0 fees, and $0 payments since then. Any tradeline showing a different or growing balance is inaccurate and can be disputed.

EOne debt may be reported several times

The original creditor, NRS, and ARS may each report the same account. Only one can show a balance. After a sale, the original tradeline should show $0 and "transferred/sold." Duplicate balances are a strong, documented dispute.

FThe deadlines on the letters don't end your rights

The May 24 and August 7 dates only govern when a collector must pause collection. Under the FCRA, you can dispute with the bureaus at any time. Once you've disputed a debt, the collector must report it as disputed.

03 · How we remove them

Four routes, strongest first

We run routes 1–3 together for Kelly now. Route 4 only applies to what's still standing after the first three.

Route 1

Proof of ownership and validation

Certified letters to Asset Recovery Solutions and Mosaic Funding X LLC. We demand the signed contract, the full assignment chain from WebBank, the charge-off date, and an itemized payment history, with duplicate-account questions for Kelly's two accounts. Unverifiable debts can't be collected or reported as valid.

Can end the debt
Route 2

Credit bureau disputes (FCRA §611 / §623)

Specific, documented inaccuracies to Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion, and directly to each furnisher: duplicate reporting, wrong balances or dates, missing "disputed" codes, and identity mismatches. Round 2 demands the bureau's method of verification, followed by a CFPB complaint where warranted.

Can delete the tradeline
Route 3

Solar-loan legal review

Mosaic loans are the subject of consumer lawsuits nationwide. If your contracts include the FTC "Holder Rule" notice, any claims you have against the solar installer can also be raised against whoever owns the loan. That applies if the sales pitch, the promised savings, or the installation was misrepresented. A consumer attorney reviews this, often at no cost.

Can reduce the balance
Route 4

Negotiate what survives

This applies only to what is fully verified after routes 1–3. Debt buyers often purchase portfolios for a fraction of face value, so our first ask is deletion in exchange for payment. Otherwise we settle at 20–40% with "paid in full" reporting and a lien release, in writing, before any money moves.

Last resort
Two honest cautions

Balances this large can lead to a lawsuit. In Utah, collectors generally have 6 years to sue on a written contract. If either of you receives court papers, call us the same day. A missed response deadline means an automatic judgment. Also, accurate and fully verified information can't be forced off a report. It ages off 7 years from the first delinquency, which is why routes 1–3 come before any payment.

06 · Before you pay anyone

Eight rules that protect any settlement

Don't pay or promise anything by phone

Collectors will call. Say "please put everything in writing" and hang up. A verbal promise can be used against you, and so can a small "good faith" payment.

Get it in writing first

You need a signed agreement with the exact amount, the due date, and the words "settled in full" before a single dollar moves.

Ask for deletion first

Debt buyers can agree to delete the tradeline. If they won't, "paid in full, $0 balance" is the fallback. The reporting language goes into the agreement.

Confirm who owns the debt

With two collectors and two claimed owners so far, the agreement must come from the verified owner. Otherwise a later buyer could come after the same balance.

Release the solar lien

Solar loans often carry a UCC fixture filing on the home. The agreement must require a UCC termination within 30 days of payment.

Never give bank access

Pay by cashier's check or a one-time card payment. No ACH authorizations and no post-dated checks.

Plan for the 1099-C

Forgiven debt over $600 can be reported to the IRS as income. At these balances, that could be a six-figure amount. The insolvency exclusion (Form 982) can remove it. Talk to your CPA before you settle.

Keep the paper trail

Keep every letter, green card, agreement, and proof of payment for 7 years. Recheck all 3 reports 45 days after any resolution.
