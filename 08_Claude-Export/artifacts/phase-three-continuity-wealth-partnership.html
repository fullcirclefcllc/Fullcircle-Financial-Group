What wealthy families do that others don't

Ten principles. Tap each to see the principle and what Full Circle does about it.

The difference between earning money and keeping it is almost never income. It is structure, discipline, and a refusal to touch the thing that produces the income. What follows is the pattern, stated plainly.

Wealth that lasts more than one generation is almost always wealth that lives on its yield. The capital itself is treated as untouchable — a machine that produces income — and the household is built to run on what the machine produces rather than on the machine itself. Spending principal is a one-way door: every dollar withdrawn also removes every dollar it would have earned for the rest of your life. Full Circle builds the income engine — entities that produce, policies that accumulate, and business credit that funds growth without liquidating anything you own.
Personal ownership exposes an asset to personal liability, personal creditors, public probate, and a lawsuit against you individually. Wealthy families hold assets through entities and trusts, so what is exposed is a membership interest rather than the underlying property, and what passes at death passes privately. Delivered in Phases 1 and 2 — PurttyWise, WiseGuy above it, and the revocable trust. What remains is funding each asset into the structure, which is where most families stop.
A holding company above the operating company, a trust above the holding company. The operating business takes the risk; the holding entity owns the value; the trust decides where it goes. This is why a lawsuit against the operating business rarely reaches the family's actual wealth. Already structured. WiseGuy Enterprises sits above PurttyWise; the trust sits above both once the membership interests are assigned.
Selling an appreciated asset triggers tax and ends the compounding. Borrowing against it keeps the asset working while releasing cash. This is a genuine advantage — and it carries real risk that gets glossed over: interest accrues whether or not the asset performs, and if the collateral falls in value or the loan outgrows it, the lender can force liquidation at the worst possible moment. Leverage is a tool with an edge on both sides. Full Circle builds the business credit and banking relationships that make borrowing available at reasonable terms — and will tell you when a deal does not support the debt.
Income-producing assets — rental fleets, operating businesses, real property, book of business — rather than assets that only appreciate on paper or depreciate in the driveway. The test is simple: does it send money every month, or does it only cost money every month? The vehicle rental and wholesale car lines are exactly this category. Full Circle structures the fleet entity, the financing, and the driver agreements.
The wealthy do not file taxes in April; they design for them in January. Entity election, reasonable compensation, depreciation timing, retirement plan selection, and the order in which income is recognized are decided in advance, with the CPA in the room before the transaction rather than after it. Full Circle coordinates with your CPA — Tony was introduced for this reason — and builds the entity and record structure that makes planning possible. Full Circle does not give tax advice.
Insurance is how wealthy families make sure a single event does not undo twenty years of building. Life coverage creates liquidity exactly when an estate is least liquid — so heirs are not forced to sell the business or the property to pay what comes due. Disability coverage protects the earner, which in most families is the actual asset. A policy is already prepared and awaiting signature. It remains the one open item from Phase 1, and the trust's beneficiary design depends on it.
Written roles, scheduled meetings, minutes, a stated purpose for the money, and a decision process that survives the death of whoever currently makes the decisions. Families that skip this rarely make it past the second generation — not because the money runs out, but because nobody agreed what it was for. Included in Foundation Care — annual minutes, resolutions, successor trustee review, and a structured quarterly conversation about direction rather than paperwork.
An attorney, a CPA, an insurance professional, and a structuring advisor who talk to each other. Wealth is lost in the gaps between advisors who each did their own piece correctly and never compared notes — the trust that was drafted beautifully and never funded, the entity that was formed and never elected. Full Circle holds the center of that team, coordinates the pieces, and makes sure nothing sits in a gap. Jenkins Law Firm, Tony on tax, and Dan on the investment side are already connected to this file.
Heirs who have never been taught how the machine works tend to dismantle it. Wealthy families bring children into the conversation early, give them small responsibilities, and explain the reasoning behind the structure rather than only the outcome. Full Circle provides the education materials and will run a family session covering the structure, the trust, and what each role actually requires.

Principal, yield, and other people's money

The three-bucket discipline, and an honest account of leverage.

The rule

Principal is untouchable. Yield is what you live on. Growth is funded by borrowed money against assets that service the debt themselves. Families that keep wealth across generations follow that order almost without exception, and the families that lose it are usually the ones who funded growth — or lifestyle — out of the principal.

BucketWhat it isWhat it's for
PrincipalThe capital base — invested assets, policy cash value, retained business equityNever spent. Only reinvested.
YieldInterest, dividends, rents, distributions, renewalsLifestyle, reinvestment, and funding the next acquisition
OPMBusiness credit, bank lines, seller financing, investor capital, vendor termsAcquiring income-producing assets that cover their own payments

What OPM actually is

Other people's money means financing acquisitions with capital that is not yours, so your own principal stays invested and compounding. The legitimate sources, roughly in order of cost:

SourceTypical useWhat it costs you
Vendor net-30 termsInventory, supplies, operating floatNothing, if paid on time
Business credit cardsShort-term working capital, ad spendInterest if carried; fees
Equipment and fleet financingVehicles that generate rental incomeInterest; the asset secures itself
Business lines of creditSeasonal gaps, opportunity capitalInterest; often a personal guarantee early on
Seller financingAcquiring an existing business or propertyNegotiated; often the cheapest capital available
Investor capitalGrowth that debt cannot coverA share of revenue or equity
The rule that keeps leverage from becoming the thing that ruins you: borrow only for assets that service their own debt. A vehicle that rents for more than its payment is leverage working. A line of credit covering payroll in a slow month is a warning sign, not a strategy. And a personal guarantee means the entity structure above does not protect you on that particular debt — which is exactly why the goal is to build business credit strong enough to borrow without one.
