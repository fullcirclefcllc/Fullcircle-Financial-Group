Why you need it

A business that stands on its own

An entity on paper is a start. A structure that is set up and kept up is what does the work.

Separation

Your business has its own name, tax ID, bank account and records. Your money and the company's money stop mixing, which makes your books, your taxes and your applications cleaner.

Liability protection

A properly formed and maintained LLC can help keep business debts and claims apart from your personal assets. That protection has limits, and it depends on keeping formalities and not commingling funds.

Credibility with lenders

Lenders, vendors and bureaus look for a real company: an entity in good standing, an EIN, a business bank account, and the same name, address and phone everywhere.

Tax options for your CPA

How the business is set up affects how it can be taxed. An LLC can choose how it's taxed, and an S corporation election is one option some owners discuss. That decision belongs with your CPA.

When you need it

The moments that call for structure

You don't need a holding company on day one. These are the stages when it's worth a structure review.

  • Stage 1Starting out

    Form it right the first time. Pick the entity, file it, get the EIN and open the business bank account before the first invoice goes out.

  • Stage 2Adding a partner

    Put ownership in writing. Who owns what, who decides, and what happens if someone leaves belongs in the operating agreement before money changes hands.

  • Stage 3Buying property or equipment

    Decide where the asset should live. Some owners hold vehicles, equipment or real estate in a separate company and lease it to the operating business.

  • Stage 4Getting ready for funding

    Clean up before you apply. Lenders check that the entity is in good standing and that your name, address and phone match across records.

  • Stage 5Hiring

    Run payroll through the business. Employees mean payroll taxes, insurance and contracts, which should sit with the operating company, not with you personally.

FAQ

Questions clients ask

Do I need a holding company?

Not always. Many businesses start with a single LLC and add layers later, when there are assets to protect, partners, or more than one line of business. The Structure Builder above shows how the layers fit. Your CPA and attorney should decide what fits your situation.

Can you help with my Arizona LLC?

Yes. We guide the formation or clean-up of your entity in Arizona and track each filing step in your portal. We work alongside your CPA and attorney and don't give legal or tax advice ourselves.

My LLC is a mess. Do I have to start over?

Usually not. Many issues, like a lapsed filing, an outdated address or a missing operating agreement, can be corrected. The entity review tells us what needs fixing and in what order.

Does a single-member LLC need an operating agreement?

It's a good idea. Arizona doesn't have you file it with the state, but banks and lenders often ask for one, and it documents that the business is run as its own entity.

Does an EIN cost anything?

No. The IRS issues EINs free of charge. Be careful with websites that charge for something the IRS provides for free.

Is an S corporation a different entity from an LLC?

An S corporation is a tax election, not a separate kind of company you form. An LLC can elect how it is taxed. Whether an election makes sense is a question for your CPA.

How long does it take?

It depends on state processing, the IRS, your bank and how quickly documents come in. We don't promise timelines. You get status updates on a regular schedule, including while we wait on a filing.
