Slide 02 · Starting position

The business exists. The volume does not.

Four divisions, live and licensed: protection, structure, leverage, and scale.

Carrier appointments with North American, F&G, and National Life Group. An active real estate license. A proven service methodology delivering entity structures, trusts, credit builds, and insurance placement. Roughly $8,000 per month collected today.

What is missing is throughput. Clients arrive one at a time through referral. There is no paid acquisition running, no dispute production system, and no fulfillment staff — so every new client consumes founder hours that should be spent selling.

That is a cash constraint, not a capability constraint. It is the only thing this capital is for.

Slide 10 · Three ways to structure it

Same capital. Different risk pricing.

$100,000 at 12% APR, 48 months, quarterly

  • Interest-only $3,000 per quarter for two quarters, then $8,852 per quarter
  • Total paid to you: $129,900
  • Most certain outcome for you, cheapest structure for the company
  • Fixed obligation regardless of results — the only structure that can strain a slow quarter
  • First-position lien plus personal guarantee

Staged $40k / $35k / $25k, paid per closed deal, capped at 1.5× drawn

  • Total paid to you at full draw: $150,000
  • Only $40,000 exposed until the funnel is proven with live data
  • Auditable deal by deal, with a $9,000 annual floor beneath it
  • Nothing owed after the cap; no residual claim on the company
  • You earn a premium over the fixed note for carrying volume risk

25% non-voting interest in a single division

  • Priority Medical Concierge or the water filtration line — never the parent company
  • Does not touch insurance renewals, real estate income, or future divisions
  • Company call option to repurchase at 2.5× trailing twelve-month net
  • Listed last because it is permanent and uncapped. Offered only if ownership is a requirement.

Cost comparison, stated plainly

The fixed note costs the company $29,900. Participation costs $50,000, because you are absorbing the risk that volume misses. Division equity costs a permanent share of earnings with no ceiling — which is why it is third rather than first.

Slide 11 · What no structure fixes

Risks that remain

Listed because a deck that omitted them would not be worth trusting.

Business risk

  • Execution. Roughly $8,000 per month collected today and the production system is not yet built. This is the dominant risk and no covenant reduces it.
  • Unproven acquisition math. A 40% miss on cost per lead or conversion extends every timeline here materially.
  • Founder dependency. Revenue runs through one licensed operator. Death is insured. Extended disability is not.
  • Regulatory. Credit repair is governed by CROA and state statutes. Enforcement or a rule change could compress the front door.

Structural limits

  • Collateral value. A lien on receivables and a client book realizes a fraction of book value in distress. It improves recovery; it does not assure it.
  • Guarantee limits. Only as strong as the guarantor's balance sheet at enforcement, and enforcement is slow and costly.
  • Illiquidity. No secondary market. Capital is committed for the term.
  • Related party. You are a current client of the firm and have been advised by it. Disclosed here; review with your own counsel.

Slide 12 · Path to closing

Six steps, in order

  1. Diligence package. Twelve months of profit and loss, bank statements, client roster, carrier appointments, system build scope, and the guarantor's personal financial statement.
  2. Existing engagement settled separately. The open Phase 2 balance closes on its own terms and is not netted against, credited toward, or commingled with this capital.
  3. Independent counsel on your side. Your attorney, not the firm's, reviewing every document.
  4. Definitions locked in writing. What counts as a closed transaction, how trailing revenue is measured at each gate, refund and chargeback netting, audit rights, reserve testing, reporting format.
  5. Executed and filed. Participation agreement, UCC-1, personal guarantee, collateral assignment, conflict disclosure, Regulation D with notice filings in Nevada and Arizona.
  6. First tranche funded. $40,000 into escrow on a 30-day disbursement schedule. First reconciled statement and payment 30 days after quarter end.
