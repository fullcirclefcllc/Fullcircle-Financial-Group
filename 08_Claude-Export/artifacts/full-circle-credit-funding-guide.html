How to use personal credit

  • Pay every account on time. Set autopay for the minimum, then pay more by hand.
  • Keep balances under 30% of each limit, and under 10% before a big application.
  • Keep old accounts open. Age helps.
  • Apply only when you need to. Each hard inquiry dips the score a little.
  • Check all three reports. Dispute what's inaccurate, outdated or unverifiable, and nothing that's true.
Chapter 3 · Build it

Business credit is a separate file

Your business can have its own credit history, tied to its EIN instead of your Social Security number. Lenders and vendors check it before they extend terms.

The business credit ladder

  1. FoundationLLC or corporation in good standing, EIN, business address and phone, website and domain email, business bank account. The same name, address and phone everywhere.
  2. Vendor net-30 accountsStarter vendors that report to D&B. Small real purchases, paid early. This creates the first Paydex.
  3. Store and retail creditOffice, hardware and warehouse store accounts once the file shows a track record.
  4. Fleet and business cardsFuel cards and business cards that underwrite the business file, some without a personal guarantee.
  5. Bank lines and loansLines of credit and term loans, which look at revenue, time in business and bank history too.
Chapter 4 · Protect it

Why no personal guarantee matters

A personal guarantee (PG) is your promise to repay if the business can't. Sign one and the debt can follow you home. Build business credit the right way and, over time, more accounts will rely on the business file instead.

Chapter 6 · Fund it

Funding, and how to use it

Funding is a tool for growth, not free money. Match the product to the job, and know what every application costs before you apply.

TypeWhat it isBest forWatch for
0% intro cardsCredit cards with an introductory 0% rateShort-term growth you can pay off inside the promoRate jumps after the promo; each application is a hard inquiry
Line of creditDraw what you need, pay interest on what you useCash-flow gaps, inventoryVariable rates, renewal terms
Term loanA lump sum repaid on a scheduleEquipment, expansion, one-time projectsOrigination fees, prepayment terms
SBA loanBank loan partly backed by the SBALarger, longer-term needsPaperwork, time to close, usually a PG
Equipment financingThe equipment secures the loanVehicles, machines, techTotal cost over the term

Rules for using funding well

  • Put it toward something that produces revenue.
  • Write the payoff plan before you apply, with dates.
  • Ask exactly what will be applied for in your name, how many hard inquiries, and every fee.
  • Pay a funding company after approval, not before.
  • Keep card balances low after funding so your scores hold up.
