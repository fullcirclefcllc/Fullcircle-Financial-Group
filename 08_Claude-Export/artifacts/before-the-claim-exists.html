01 — Positioning

What You Are Actually Selling

Not entities. Not trusts. You are selling the discipline that makes someone else's documents survive contact with a courtroom — and that is a product nobody in this industry is selling properly.

The documents are commodity. The operating discipline is not.

An attorney can draft a holding company and three subsidiaries in a week. What no attorney is set up to do — and what almost no client does on their own — is verify that each entity has a separate bank account, that capital contributions were documented, that annual reports were filed in three states, that intercompany leases exist and were signed, and that nothing has been run through a personal checking account since March.

That gap is where structures die. Veil piercing turns on unity of interest: commingling, treating entity assets as personal, failure to maintain records, undercapitalization. Every one of those is an operations failure, not a drafting failure — and every one of them is unambiguously not the practice of law.

So the business is: diagnose exposure as fact, close the insurance gap first, hand counsel a file that starts at analysis, execute the filings, and then maintain the structure annually, forever. Formation is the transaction. Maintenance is the business.

The sentence that sells this without crossing the line

"We don't decide what structure you need — your attorney does, and we'll hand them a file so complete it saves you money. What we do is make sure that once it exists, it's still standing in five years. Most people's isn't, and they find out at the worst possible moment." Every clause of that is true, none of it is legal advice, and it reframes the entire purchase from a one-time document fee to an ongoing relationship.

02 — Monetization

The Four Doors

Same machine, four buyers. Door 1 builds the compliance record; Doors 2 through 4 sell the system that record proves. Door 4 carries risk the other divisions' licensing doors do not.

One structural note that outranks all four doors. Arizona permits non-lawyer ownership of a legal-services entity through Alternative Business Structure certification, and permits fee-sharing among ABS participants. FCFG is an Arizona firm. An ABS would collapse the wall this entire build-out is designed around — inside Arizona. It does not license nationwide operation, and Texas and California both restrict working with ABS-affiliated attorneys. Investigate it with counsel before committing to any of the four doors above, because the answer changes the economics of all of them.

The proof engine

This division's proof is operational, not anecdotal: on-time filing rate, separate-account rate, maintenance renewal rate, and the number of entities under management. Those numbers are provable, they are boring in exactly the way that builds trust with professionals, and no competitor publishes them because no competitor tracks them.

What you can never say

Judgment-proof. Creditor-proof. "Your assets can never be touched." "We'll make you invisible." Any promise that a structure defeats a specific creditor. Any implication that you decide what structure someone needs. And any quasi-legal title — no "trust advisor", no "specialist", nothing with "legal" in the name. Those claims are false on the law and they convert a regulatory inquiry into a fraud count.

05 — The org chart of a machine

How This Division Is Actually Staffed

Five pods, and one of them exists to stop the other four from practicing law. Twenty agents, five people — with the Maintenance pod carrying the recurring revenue.

The rule that keeps this legal

Agents gather, map, assemble, file, track and remind. Attorneys decide, draft, and explain. Humans in your firm approve and sign. Every gate approval is logged with the approver, the timestamp, and the version — and the compensation logic never references a legal fee in either direction. In a division regulated by four different bodies at once, that log is the product's real warranty.

The point of all of it

Draw the line precisely. Then build a real business on the correct side of it.

Everyone in this space either crosses the line because nobody drew it, or hovers so far from it that they sell nothing but introductions. There is a third option, and it happens to be the more valuable one: own the diagnosis, own the insurance layer, own the execution, and own the maintenance — the part that decides whether any of the documents were worth drafting. That business is defensible, recurring, and genuinely useful, which is more than most of this industry can say.
