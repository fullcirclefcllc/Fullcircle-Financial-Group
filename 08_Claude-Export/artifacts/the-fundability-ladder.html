01 — Findings

The Verdict

Business credit is the most oversold service in the financial services industry. The opportunity is in being the operator who tells the truth about it — and then delivers something that actually works.

Primary findingThe tier ladder does not lead where the marketing says it leads

The industry sells a staircase: net-30 vendors → store cards → fleet cards → bank credit, all unlocked by a PAYDEX score. The first two rungs are real. The top rung is not. Cash credit in this country arrives from exactly three places, and none of them is a business credit score: $25,000–$50,000 sitting in a business bank account (Ramp, Brex), a 680+ personal FICO run through card stacking at 9–15% commission and a personal guarantee, or a lender underwriting revenue and debt service coverage.

That is not a reason to abandon the service. It is a reason to build the only version of it that survives an FTC inquiry: separate purchasing power from cash credit in every client-facing view, disclose the personal-credit basis of placement in writing before the client enters the funding stage, and price the program on the foundation work — which genuinely takes six to twelve months and which almost nobody does properly.

Conflating purchasing power with cash credit is the exact fact pattern the FTC charged in FTC v. Seek Capital (November 2025) — a $48.28 million judgment and a permanent lifetime ban on marketing credit, loans, or financing. Read that complaint as your prohibited-claims list, not as someone else's problem.

What a business credit program genuinely delivers

A clean, matched, fundable business identity. A file at D&B, Experian, and Equifax that actually exists. Verified reporting tradelines. Purchasing power at real suppliers. A documented revenue and banking history. And a client who is legitimately underwritable in twelve months instead of guessing in five years.

What it cannot deliver, ever

$100,000 in thirty days. Cash credit with no personal guarantee for a business with no balance and no revenue. A PAYDEX score before three trade experiences from two suppliers have reported. A credit profile that is genuinely 'separate from' the owner's personal credit when the terminal product carries a personal guarantee.

Two facts changed the playbook this year, and most operators haven't caught up. The SBA sunset the FICO SBSS prescreen for 7(a) Small Loans effective March 1, 2026 — replaced by full credit analysis with a DSCR floor of 1.1:1, two months of bank statements, and the credit-elsewhere test. Any curriculum module built around 'get your SBSS to 165' is obsolete. Separately, FinCEN permanently exempted domestic entities from BOI reporting (final rule effective August 14, 2026) — delete that step from your entity workflow.

The rule that makes this division different from the competition

Score vendors by destination bureau and verified reporting, never by tier or count. A tradeline that does not land at D&B, Experian, Equifax or SBFE is a cost the client paid for and a promise you did not keep. Build an automated verification checkpoint into the workflow — both Nav Prime and D&B's own CreditBuilder have documented histories of billing clients while tradelines failed to report, and D&B's version of that became an FTC action in 2022.

The three routes to cash — say all three out loud

Balance-based: $25,000 in a linked business account gets a Ramp card; $50,000 or venture backing gets Brex. No credit check, no guarantee — but the money has to already be there. Stacking: 680+ personal FICO, 9–15% commission, personal guarantee, post-promotional APRs of 15–35%. Underwritten: revenue and debt service coverage. For SBA 7(a) small loans since March 2026 that means DSCR of at least 1.1:1, two months of bank statements, and the credit-elsewhere test — with no SBSS shortcut.

Capacity math

Foundation and sequencing work is roughly 6–9 hours per client across a twelve-month engagement, concentrated in months 0–4. One trained coordinator running a documented vendor ledger handles 60–80 active files. With ANVIL, CAIRN and PROBE carrying the checklist, tracking and verification, that ceiling moves toward 150. Your first hire is the coordinator, not a closer.

Where operators actually price

Self-serve course and vendor lists run $997–$1,497. Coaching and premium tiers run $1,997–$2,997. Full done-for-you programs run $3,000–$4,000. Recurring monitoring is a $30–$100 monthly passthrough. Funding placement — the part where the money concentrates — runs 9–15% of the approved limit.

There are no industry benchmarks. Build your own.

No audited or association data exists for business credit consulting margins. Every figure circulating is self-reported marketing from people selling licenses. Instrument the metrics that actually matter to you: activation rate to first verified tradeline, days to the PAYDEX gate, percentage reaching three experiences from two suppliers, placement rate, average approved limit, and refund rate. Those numbers are also your substantiation record.

Planning estimates at settings you chose — not forecasts, and not claims you may repeat in marketing without a substantiation record behind them.

The sentences that ended a company

FTC v. Seek Capital (November 2025) produced a $48,280,328 judgment and a permanent lifetime ban on marketing credit, loans, financing, debt relief or credit repair. The specific misrepresentations charged: claiming special relationships with lenders; charging thousands of dollars merely to open credit cards while marketing it as business financing; claiming the products had "line of credit capability"; claiming favorable terms including 0% APR; claiming no fees until funding was received; claiming the process would not harm credit scores; and contract terms barring negative reviews. Every one of those is a sentence a business credit consultant might say casually on a Tuesday. Put them in the prohibited-claims list and put the list in front of every closer.

Design rule that removes most of the risk in one move

Never let a human type a financial figure into this system. Revenue, time in business, employee count, balances — all derived from an uploaded bank statement or formation document, all locked, all attributable to a source file. It costs a day to build and it makes the single most serious criminal exposure in this division structurally difficult to commit.

13 — Closing

Closing

What this audit is really saying

The business credit industry sells a staircase that stops three floors below where it points. You can build the same staircase and add the same disclaimer everyone else buries — or you can be the operator who says out loud what actually unlocks money, builds the foundation properly over twelve months, and proves every claim with data.

The second version is harder to sell in the first conversation and impossible to compete with by the third. It is also the only version that survives a regulator reading your website. Build the vendor ledger, verify every tradeline, never let anyone type a revenue figure, and let the honesty be the marketing.
