Step 5

The continuity offer

One-time services make you start from zero every month. This is the piece that compounds. Set the membership price, what it costs you to serve one member each month, and how long members stay.

How to set it up
  1. Make it the exit of every service. Every one-time engagement ends with enrollment into the membership at completion. That is the single highest-converting moment you have, and it costs nothing to ask.
  2. Give it a job the client feels monthly. Monitoring and a monthly report, a quarterly strategy call, dispute rounds or vendor pushes while active, and first access to funding. If a member can't say what they got this month, they cancel.
  3. Bill it on the card that's already on file. Same processor, same day each month, in GoHighLevel. Dunning on failed cards recovers more revenue than any new campaign.
  4. Build it once, deliver it to everyone. The monthly report and the group call are one-to-many. Cost to serve should barely move as members grow — that's what makes this the most profitable line you'll own.
  5. Track one number weekly: net member change. Adds minus cancels. Everything else is noise.
Step 6

What to pay Adam

Model the consultant's comp against the lift he actually produces. Pay for the increase, not for the business you already built.

Structure it this way
  1. Pay on the increase, never on total revenue. Set a baseline from your trailing three months and write it into the agreement. He earns a share of what's above it. A percentage of everything hands him money your existing book already produces.
  2. Use gross profit, not revenue. A share of revenue can pay him on a sale you lost money delivering.
  3. Put a term and a cap on it. Twelve to twenty-four months, with a stated ceiling. Consulting engagements end; a share without an end date becomes a permanent partner you never chose.
  4. Keep it a contract, not ownership. Revenue share is a payment term. Equity gives up control and a vote forever, and it's a much harder thing to undo when the fit isn't right.
  5. Start smaller than feels generous. A modest retainer plus a share of the lift, with a review at ninety days. If he's as good as advertised, the review is when you raise it — and you'll both have evidence.
Before you sign anything: insurance commission cannot be shared with an unlicensed person in most states — if Adam isn't appointed and licensed, your comp has to be calculated so it isn't a cut of commission. Have the agreement reviewed, and confirm the commission point with your carrier's compliance desk.
