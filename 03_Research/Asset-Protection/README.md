# Asset Protection — Research

## Core Principles
1. **Separation** — personal wealth must be legally separate from business liability
2. **Anonymity** — minimizing what is publicly traceable back to you
3. **Layers** — multiple entities make it costly for plaintiffs to pursue

## Structure Layers

```
YOU (personally)
    ↓ owns
Holding LLC (Wyoming / Nevada preferred — high privacy)
    ├── Operating LLC (where work happens, lawsuits land here)
    ├── Real Estate LLC (holds property, separate from business risk)
    └── IUL / Life Insurance Policy (protected in most states)
```

## Anonymity by State

| State | Public Member Disclosure | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wyoming | No | Best for privacy — members not listed publicly |
| New Mexico | No | No annual report required |
| Nevada | No | Strong charging order protection |
| Delaware | No | Favorable legal environment |
| Most others | Yes | Names and addresses on public record |

**Strategy:** Form holding company in Wyoming or New Mexico; operate in your home state via registered agent.

## Trust Structures

| Trust Type | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Revocable Living Trust | Estate planning — avoids probate | You retain control; not asset protection |
| Irrevocable Trust | Strong asset protection | You give up control; harder to attack |
| Land Trust | Holds real estate anonymously | Title in trust name, not yours |
| Spendthrift Trust | Protects beneficiaries from creditors | |

## Charging Order Protection
A charging order is the only remedy a creditor has against an LLC member in most states — they cannot force liquidation or take control of the entity, only receive distributions if made.

**Strongest charging order states:** Wyoming, Nevada, Delaware, Alaska
